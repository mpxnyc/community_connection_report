Data collection instrument

We designed and implemented a novel online survey instrument which captures spatial information at a resolution fine enough to analyze overall patterns in social and sexual contact in the population but coarse enough to preserve the annonymity of the survey.

This was accomplished through a user interface that resembles the google maps (r) search interface linked to the google maps API. To collect spatial data, participants were prompted to indicate all venues where they had had physical or sexual contact in a congregate setting over the preceding 4 weeks. The user interface allows the participant to search by neighborhood, place name, street address, and other markers which are admitted by the google maps search interface. When the user taps on a place in the map, a pop-up window asks them to confirm the location. If they do confirm, they receive a series of questions related to that particular venue. Once all the questions relating to a venue are answered, the user is allowed to tap on the map again to indicate a second venue, etc.

In addition, the survey instrument collected data thorugh other question formats (multiple choice, single choice, etc.). The look and feel of the data colleciton instrument was consistent with the visual identity established by the campaign. Data were stored in a custom built graph database system hosted on a secure cloud server platform.

The instrument was validated thorugh 3 rounds of user testing before data collection. Users were asked to comment on [AIRTABLE].

Measures

Participants

We solicited information on participant sociodemographics, utilization of healthcare services such as pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP), HIV treatment and vaccination against MPOX; connection to queer or transgender community through sex and socializing; and location of primary residence.

Gender identity was measured using a two-step approach: first, we solicited the participant’s current gender (), and then their sex assigned at birth (). Those whose gender identity was not concordant with the gender commonly associated with their sex assigned at birth were categorized as transgender. We used current gender to further categorize transgender people into men and women. Race was measured using a multiple selection question based on census categories (). We defined gender-race subgroup as the combination of gender identity and race. We collapsed small cells across racial groups.

Contact venues

We asked whether participants had engaged in group sex or had physical contact in a congregate setting in the four weeks before taking the survey. Among those who answered positively, we solicited the locations of the venues at which the contact happened. We refer to these locations as contact venues.

For each contact venue, participants indicated the type of place it was () and whether they had sexual (vs. non-sexual physical) contact there.

As a crude measure of the distance between the contact venue and participant residence, we categorized contact venues into those that are in the same neighborhood as the residence, the same borough but different neighborhood, or a different borough than the participant’s residence.

Spatial mixing

To understand how the contact venues and residences of different kinds of participants are clustered in space, we calculated the spatial mixing coefficient. The spatial mixing coefficient from characteristic X to characteristic Y compares the conditional probability of someone with charestic X sharing a community district with someone with characteristic Y to the marginal probability of having characteristic Y. The spatial mixing coefficient is defined as the ratio between these two probabilities minus 1.

Statistical Analysis

We tabulated participant responses to questions about healthcare access, connection with community, and demographics by report of contact. We also tabulated the characteristic of contact venues. We visualized the flow of study participants between home and contact venues using a matrix plot. We plotted this measure for subgroups defined by race-gender, sexual orientation, and age.

Hypothetical Intervention

We examined the efficiency and impact of a hypothetical intervention to prioritize community district according to the amount of contact and vaccinate those districts in order of that priorization. We assume that vaccinating a community district means rendering every individuals who lives in or has had contact in that community district unable to acquire or transmit a hypothetical pathogen.

Statistical Inference

We used the non-parametric bootstrap to calculate point estimates and 95% confidence intervals (CI). This was based on re-sampling individual participants with replacement.
