In this appendix we describe the main data objects of the MPX NYC study – the Person-place graph, the Person graph and the Place graph – as well as the statistics we used to analyze them.

The RESPND-MI/MPX NYC person-place graph is a data graph that contains all collected data. In this graph, there are two types of nodes: community districts and survey participants. Edges can only exist between a participant and a community district - they indicate that the person either lives in or attended a gathering in that community district. Person graph

The RESPND-MI/MPX NYC has study participants as nodes. A pair of nodes is connected by a weighted edge if each of the participants represented by the nodes share at least one community district in which they both had a gathering or residence. The weight of the edge is equal to the count of community districts the pair of nodes have in common.

The RESPND-MI/MPX NYC place graph has community districts as nodes. A pair of community districts is connected by a weighted edge when at least one participant has a residence or attended a gathering in both community districts. D.2 Adjacency functions

An adjacency function is a function that takes the labels for two nodes, and returns a non-negative value based on whether the nodes are connected or not, or based on the weight of the connection between the nodes. (A weight of zero indicates no connection between the nodes). We use the adjacency functions for the graphs defined above to define useful statistics.

First, we define \(\psi(i,j)\) as the adjacency function for the RESPND-MI/MPX NYC person-place graph. For person \(i\) and community \(j\):

\[ \psi(i,j) = \begin{cases} 1 &i\text{ connected to } j \\ 0 &\text{otherwise} \end{cases} \]

Then \(\psi_P(i,k)\), the adjacency function for the RESPND-MI/MPX NYC person graph is defined:

\[ \psi_P(i,k) = \sum_{j \in N_C}{\psi(i,j) \psi(k,j)} \]

Finally, \(\psi_C(j,l)\), the adjacency function for the RESPND-MI/MPX NYC place graph is defined:

\[ \psi_C(j,l) = \sum_{i \in N_P}{\psi(i,j) \psi(i,l)} \]

D.3 Measures

D.3.1 Node characteristics

Conditional participant social reach from \(i\) to \(A\) is the number of participant nodes in set \(A \subset \mathscr{N}_P\) that node \(i\) shares a community district with (either because both nodes have a residence or gathering in that community district). The measure is defined as weighted node degree in the RESPND-MI/MPX NYC person graph.

\[ R_{i\rightarrow A} = \sum_{\begin{aligned} j &\in A \end{aligned}}{\psi_p (i,j)} \]

Conditional community district social reach from \(j\) to A is defined as the number of connections the focal community district has with participants. This can be because partipants attended a gathering or have a residence in that community district. The measure is defined as the weighted node degree in the RESPND-MI/MPX NYC person-place graph.

\[ r_{j\rightarrow A} = \sum_{i \in A} {\psi (i, j)} \]

When \(A=\mathscr{N}_p\), these measures are called participant social reach (\(R_i\)), and community district social reach (\(r_j\)), respectively.

D.3.2 Network characteristics

The movement matrix is a square matrix whose \(kl^{th}\) entry, \(m(k,l)\), is defined as the count of outings from home in community district \(k\) to a gathering in community district \(l\). Say \(d^H(i)\) is the home community district of participant \(i\). Then:

\[ m(k,l) = \sum_{\begin{aligned}i \in \mathscr{N}_P \\ d^H(i) = k \end{aligned}} \psi (i,l) \]

The spatial concentration proportion \(c(k)\) quantifies the degree to which a community district is over-represented among destination community districts compared to home districts in the movement matrix. It is the difference of the margins of the movement matrix.

\[ c(k) = \frac{\sum_l{m(l,k)} - \sum_k{m(l,k)}}{\sum_{l,k}{m(l,k)}} \]

We define preference of group A for group B (for \(A, B \subset \mathscr{N}_p\)) as the average proportion of alters from group B among egos from group A.

\[ \Phi(A,B) = \frac{1}{|A|}\sum_{i \in A}{\frac{ R_{i \rightarrow B}}{R_i}} \]

The prevalence of group B is the size of the group divided by the total number of participants.

\[ \frac{|B|}{|N_p|} \]

The spatial mixing matrix is the ratio of preference to prevalence minus one. It quantifies the degree to which egos in group A preferentially live in or attend gatherings in community districts they are likely to encounter members of group B.

\[ \phi(A,B) = \frac{|N_p|}{|B|} \Phi(A,B) - 1 \]

In a given network, a connected component is a subset of nodes such that each node in the subset is reachable from any other node in the subset, and such that there are no nodes outside the subset which are reachable from a node inside it. By reachable, we mean possible to reach by tracing a path from one node to another over existing network ties.

We define the proportion in the largest connected component as:

\[ \theta_p = \frac{\max_k{|C_k|}}{|N_p|} \]

Acknowledgements: The analytic framework for this project developed over an extended period of time, beginning during my PhD dissertation (Harvard University, 2021, under the supervision of Lisa Berkman, Eric Tchetgen Tchetgen, and a former mentor I no longer contact). In particular, the analysis borrows from the central paper in my dissertation, which shows how the wealth of non-coresident extended family members is protective against mortality.

This work was part of U.S. government-funded research conducted in South Africa through the HAALSA study, which has since been defunded by the Trump administration. It is path-breaking because it shows an approach to using a large scale (over 100,000 people observed over three decades) dataset as a family network. There are many more datasets that could be analyzed using these methods. Any investigator who ever hosted a study at the Agincourt surveillance site in the north-east of South Africa, for instance, can use these methods to interpret their own data in light of family networks. It has the potential to illustrate the value of deep collaboration between U.S. and South African researchers. It is also of pedagogical value. I wrote the appendices of my dissertation in the same style as these appendices – with students and teachers of epidemiology in mind.
