D.1 Preface

The RESPND-MI/MPX NYC person-place data graph contains all study data, which are represented as node and edge characteristics. There are two types of nodes – community districts, and survey participants. Each edge in the graph connects one community district with one survey participant.

D.3 Local network structure

Spatial reach

Spatial reach of person \(i\) is the number of relations person node \(i\) has with place nodes in the person-place graph. Let \(\psi(i,j)\) be the number of edges connecting person \(i\) and place \(j\) in the person-place graph. Then:

\[ R_{i} = \sum_{\begin{aligned} j &\in \mathscr{N}_C \end{aligned}}{\psi (i,j)} \]

Social reach

Social reach of person \(i\) among subgroup \(A\) is the number of person nodes in set \(A \subset \mathscr{N}_P\) that are connected to person node \(i\) through shared places. i.e. either because both nodes have a residence or gathering in at least one common community district. Social reach of person \(i\) is defined as \(R_{i} = R_{i\rightarrow \mathscr{N}_P}\)

\[ R_{i\rightarrow A} = \sum_{\begin{aligned} j &\in A \end{aligned}}{\sum_{k \in \mathscr{N}_C}{\psi(i,k)\psi(j,k)} } \]

Social catchment

Social catchment of place \(j\) among subgroup \(A\) is defined as the number of connections the focal community district has with participants who belong to subgroup \(A \subset \mathscr{N}_P\). Social catchment of place \(j\) is defined as \(r_{j} = r_{j\rightarrow \mathscr{N}_P}\)

\[ r_{j\rightarrow A} = \sum_{i \in A} {\psi (i, j)} \]

Spatial catchment

Spatial catchment of place \(j\) via subgroup \(A\) is defined as the strength of connection between place node \(j\) and other place nodes in the graph when we consider their shared relations with person nodes in subgroup \(A\). Strength of connection between two places \(j\) and \(k\) is defined as the number of relations place nodes \(j\) and \(k\) have with common person nodes which belong to subset \(A\). The spatial catchment of place \(j\) is defined as \(r_{i\rightarrow \mathscr{N}_P} = r_{i}\)

\[ r_{j\rightarrow A} = \sum_{i \in \mathscr{N}_C} \sum_{k \in A}{\psi(k,i)\psi(k,j)} \\ \]

D.4 Global network structure

Spatial mixing

The spatial mixing coefficient from A to B quantifies the degree to which egos in group A preferentially live in or attend gatherings in community districts they are likely to encounter members of group B. It is defined as the ratio of preference to prevalence minus one.

\[ P_{A \rightarrow B} = \frac{|\mathscr{N}_p|}{|A||B|} \sum_{i \in A} P_{i \rightarrow B} - 1 \]

We define preference of person \(i\) for group $ B _p$) as the proportion of alters from group B among all alters

\[ P_{i \rightarrow B} = {\frac{ R_{i \rightarrow B}}{R_i}} \]

Social connectedness

Proportion in largest connected component: In a given network, a connected component is a subset of nodes such that each node in the subset is reachable from any other node in the subset, and such that there are no nodes outside the subset which are reachable from a node inside it. By reachable, we mean possible to reach by tracing a path from one node to another over existing network ties. Say the size of the \(k^{th}\) connected component is \(C_k\). The proportion in the largest connected component is given by: \[ \theta_p = \frac{\max_k{C_k}}{|\mathscr{N}_p|} \]

D.5 Dynamics on the network

Movement

The movement matrix is a square matrix whose \(kl^{th}\) entry, \(m(k,l)\), is defined as the count of outings from home in community district \(k\) to a gathering in community district \(l\). Say \(d^H(i)\) is the home community district of participant \(i\). \[ m(k,l) = \sum_{\begin{aligned}i \in \mathscr{N}_P \\ d^H(i) = k \end{aligned}} \psi (i,l) \]

Spatial concentration

The spatial concentration proportion \(c(k)\) quantifies the degree to which a community district is over-represented among destination community districts compared to home districts in the movement matrix. It is the difference of the margins of the movement matrix. \[ c(k) = \frac{\sum_l{m(l,k)} - \sum_k{m(l,k)}}{\sum_{l,k}{m(l,k)}} \]

D.6 Statistical inference

  • The target of inference is the person-place population graph:
    • \(\mathscr{G}^*(\mathscr{N}^*, \mathscr{E}^*, d(.))\)
  • Node set is
    • \(\mathscr{N}^* = \mathscr{N}_P^* \cup \mathscr{N}_C\)
    • \(|\mathscr{N}_P^*| = n_P^* \rightarrow \infty\)
    • \(\mathscr{N}_C = n_C\)
  • Homogeneous ego networks
    • structural equation for adjacency graph is the same for each node
    • gibbs sampler
      • f(adjacency | other adjacencies, my data)
      • f(data | adjacencies, data
  • Edges are
    • only between places and people
  • we want a functional of node and edge universe data
    • But we have a function of node and edge sample data
  • under what condition do we have unbiasedness?
    • random sample
    • conditionally random sample
  • Simulated person-place graph \(\mathscr{G}^*(\mathscr{N}^*, \mathscr{E}^*)\)
    • Nodes \(\mathscr{N}^* = \mathscr{N}_P^* \cup \mathscr{N}_C^*\)
      • Place nodes \(\mathscr{N}_C^*\): \(m\) copies of each element in \(\mathscr{N}_C\)
      • Person nodes \(\mathscr{N}_P^*\): random sample of size \(m \times |\mathscr{N}_P|\) from \(\mathscr{N}_P\) drawn with replacement
      • Since \(\mathscr{N}^*\) is comprised of re-labelled elements of \(\mathscr{N}\), \(\exists \phi : \mathscr{N}^* \rightarrow \mathscr{N}\) such that \(a \in \mathscr{N}^* \implies \phi(a) \in \mathscr{N}\) (node name mapping)
    • Edges
      • for \(a, b \in \mathscr{N}^*\): \((\phi(a), \phi(b)) \in \mathscr{E} \implies (x, a, b) \in \mathscr{E}^*\) for some \(x \in \mathbf{Z}_+\)
  • Remarks:
    • We can create simulated person graph and simulated place graph as bipartite projections of the simulated person-place graph since the latter is a bipartite graph
    • Statistical inference is conducted using the empirical distribution for each measure.
      • The empirical distribution is estimated by calculating each measures within strata defined by simulation repetition, and summarizing the sample distribution across repetitions
    • Simulation repetitions each comprise disjoint components of the simulted person-place graph

Acknowledgements: The analytic framework for this project developed over an extended period of time, beginning during my PhD dissertation at Harvard University under the supervision of Lisa Berkman, Eric Tchetgen Tchetgen, and a former mentor). In particular, the analysis borrows from the central paper in my dissertation, which shows how the wealth of non-coresident extended family members is protective against mortality.

This work was part of U.S. government-funded research conducted in South Africa through the HAALSA study, which has since been defunded by the Trump administration. It is path-breaking because it shows an approach to using a large scale (over 100,000 people observed over three decades) dataset as a family network. There are many more datasets that could be analyzed using these methods. Any investigator who ever hosted a study at the Agincourt surveillance site in the north-east of South Africa, for instance, can use these methods to interpret their own data in light of family networks. It has the potential to illustrate the value of deep collaboration between U.S. and South African researchers. It is also of pedagogical value. I wrote the appendices of my dissertation in the same style as these appendices – with students and teachers of epidemiology in mind.
